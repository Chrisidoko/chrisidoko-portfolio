## About NCAIR

The National Centre for Artificial Intelligence and Robotics (NCAIR) is one of [NITDA's](https://nitda.gov.ng/) special purpose vehicles, created to promote research and development on emerging technologies and their practical application in areas of Nigerian national interest.

I joined as a Graduate Engineering Intern during my [NYSC](https://portal.nysc.org.ng/nysc1/) service year. There I deepened my skills in research, Python development and embedded systems, and applied them to real-world projects. Two of those projects are below.

---

## Smart Assistive Eyewear

For a visually impaired person, an obstacle at head or chest height is easy to miss with a cane alone. Together with a fellow intern, I built a wearable prototype that helps: a pair of glasses that detects obstacles and talks to the wearer.

The eyewear combines **ultrasonic sensors** for obstacle detection with **character recognition**, and gives the wearer **real-time audio feedback** to help them navigate.

### My part

I worked on this project with a co-intern and carried most of the build:

- **Programming the Arduino**, the microcontroller that reads the sensors and decides when to alert the wearer.
- **Building the prototype**, turning loose components into something that works on a person's face.
- **Packaging**, making the electronics wearable as a single piece of eyewear.

### A familiar voice

Most devices alert you with a beep. A beep tells you *something* is there, but not what to do about it. For this prototype I recorded **my own voice** as the notification sound, so the wearer hears a human voice guiding them instead of an anonymous tone.

## Solar Tracker System

I was also part of a multidisciplinary team of interns that designed and built a **Solar Tracker System**. A fixed solar panel only faces the sun directly for part of the day; our prototype was designed to **follow the sunlight**, turning the panel to keep it facing the sun.

It built on the embedded work I'd started at university, where I built a stress-detection wearable using a galvanic skin response (GSR) sensor with IoT connectivity. This time I was working on hardware as part of a multidisciplinary team.

---

## What I took away

Most of my work is software, where you can undo almost anything. Hardware is less forgiving: wiring, power and physical fit all matter, and a prototype only counts if it works on a real person. NCAIR taught me to build for the person who will actually use the thing. That lesson carries straight back into the software I build today.
